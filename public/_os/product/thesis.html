Product thesis — the thoughtful person's guide to running their own AI assistant

Product thesis — the thoughtful person's guide to running their own AI assistant

Apr 24, 20268 min read

This is a rough sketch of the first real thing that could come out of the vault. It is written for Hiro, to argue with. If any part of it doesn’t fit, kill it.

The core bet: Hiro’s edge is taste and philosophy applied to the lived practice of running a personal AI assistant. The product is the writing, the templates, and the opinionated recipe — not a framework, library, or startup. See hiro-profile for why this framing instead of a more ambitious one.

The problem

Everyone running a personal AI setup in 2026 — whether on Claude Code, OpenClaw, Cursor, or anything adjacent — hits the same wall after the first week:

  • The assistant starts from near-zero on every new task.
  • You re-teach context, conventions, preferences, vocabulary, over and over.
  • You accumulate half-written CLAUDE.md files, stray skills, ad-hoc hooks, and a vague sense that your setup should be getting smarter over time but isn’t.
  • You know the primitives exist. You just don’t know how to hold them — what goes in memory, what goes in skills, what goes in hooks, what goes in project docs, and how they should relate.

What’s missing isn’t another framework. The primitives are already here. What’s missing is a design philosophy and a lived practice — somebody who has sat with the real running system for months, paid attention to what broke and what compounded, and written it down with enough taste that other people can copy the pattern.

Hiro has already been doing this in private. Kaizen Mechanism.md is the spec. CC - Foundational Setup Ideas.md is the philosophy. CC - Foundational Setup Checklist.md is the open questions. All that’s missing is the step where it gets written down carefully enough to hand to someone else.

The insight

“CC should learn from everything, but reason with only what has been distilled.” — from CC - Foundational Setup Ideas.md

This one line is the thesis for the whole product. The personal AI assistant is not a tool that improves autonomously. It is a tool that distills, with human review at every step. The human’s job is not to instruct the assistant. It is to run a Kaizen loop over the assistant’s own behavior, keeping what compounds and deleting what doesn’t. The assistant’s job is to make that loop cheap and visible.

The product is the practice of doing this well, documented by someone whose taste is trustworthy. Not a library. Not a framework. A way of working that readers can copy.

This is the difference between “here is a React library” and “here is how to build React apps.” The second is more valuable for an earlier audience.

What v0 ships

Four pieces, in order of how much work they are and how soon they can exist:

1. The rendered vault (ships as soon as we decide)

Hiro’s vault, published as a navigable site. Wikilinks become hyperlinks. The graph is browsable. Readers walk through his mind in the order their curiosity takes them. This is the manifesto — not a distilled essay, but the whole thinking, visible in its rough, unfinished state. Part of the appeal is that it is unfinished. See prototype for the rendering approach.

Value: zero to Hiro (the content already exists), high to readers (nobody else is publishing design-engineer-grade AI-assistant thinking in raw form).

2. One flagship essay — “Clarity is the new limit” (1–2 weeks of work)

A single sharpened essay extracted from Evolving Clarity.md and AI-First.md, under Hiro’s name, published on his own domain or Substack. 1200 words, tight, quotable, designed to be shared. The line “the new limit is clarity” is the hook; the body is the lived proof.

Value: entry point for readers who would never open a vault. Produces the first 500 subscribers.

3. The CC Starter Kit (3–6 weeks of work)

An opinionated, free, downloadable bundle that lets another power user run a CC-shaped setup of their own. Concretely:

  • A CLAUDE.md template encoding Hiro’s canonical rules (the Writing rules, the H - Prime values translated into instructions, the Kaizen loop as a skill)
  • A minimal skill pack: reflect-on-session, propose-improvements, distill-memory, maybe 3 more
  • A hook script for Claude Code’s Stop event that runs the reflection prompt after each session and produces the list of suggested diffs Hiro described in Kaizen Mechanism.md
  • A small docs/ folder explaining the why for each piece — with internal links back to the original vault notes
  • A README with an “install in 60 seconds” path

It is not software Hiro has to maintain as infrastructure. It is a starter — something a reader forks, adopts, edits, and makes their own. Think of it the way Tailwind UI is a starter, not a framework.

Value: this is the thing that travels. Power users share starter kits. It’s also the piece with the most design-engineer work per unit, which is exactly the shape Hiro thrives in.

4. A paid tier (after traction)

Once the essay and the kit have found an audience, there are natural paid products:

  • A weekly/biweekly essay behind a 8–15/mo Substack paywall
  • A “pro” starter kit variant with more skills, templates, and walkthroughs — $49 one-time or subscription
  • Eventually: a book. Personal AI: A Design Philosophy. Could be self-published on Leanpub first, then a traditional deal if the audience is there.

Value: this is where the money shows up, but it’s dependent on the above two earning trust first. Do not lead with it.

Why this wins

Shippable by Hiro as he already is. No new skills. No new tools. The starter kit is design-engineer work — bundles, docs, opinionated defaults, clean aesthetics. The essay is writing. The vault site is publishing what already exists. Every piece of this project uses muscles Hiro already has, and none of it requires him to become a founder, a salesperson, or a public figure.

Unique at the taste + philosophy layer. Plenty of people are building AI tools. Almost nobody with real design taste is publicly writing the design philosophy for how to run one well. The taste gap is real. Maggie Appleton is the closest comparable, and she isn’t deep in personal-assistant setup specifically.

Compatible with the reserved profile. Writer-builders can stay text-only for years before they ever need to appear on a stage. The audience comes to them, not the other way around. This is the only money path from the vault that honors the Self-Observation.md reality of Hiro needing equanimity.

Builds on rather than against the ecosystem. OpenClaw, Claude Code, Cursor are the platforms. We ride on top of them, we teach their power users how to use them better, we become a beloved voice inside those communities. No zero-sum competition. The ecosystems want someone doing this work.

Slow → fast. The writer-builder model is slow until it isn’t. The first 6 months are quiet — 500 subscribers, a few hundred kit downloads. The next 12 months, if the taste is right, can compound into low-six-figures of recurring income and an audience big enough for a book advance, a sponsored newsletter, or an advisory retainer on Hiro’s own terms. No VC arc. No acqui-hire panic. No “what are we raising for.” Just work that travels.

Why not the other paths

  • Not a framework/library. OpenClaw and the agent ecosystems already own this layer. Competing there means losing.
  • Not consulting. hiro-profile section: no prior shipping credibility, wrong personality profile for sales. Covered.
  • Not a venture-backed company. Can’t honor the equanimity requirement. Would break Hiro before it funded him.
  • Not a product for non-technical users (the SaaC vision from Income Ideas.md). Too ambitious for v0, too far from the current audience, better as a v3 dream.

What this requires from Hiro

Minimum viable commitment, if he wanted to try it:

  1. ~2 days to decide if this framing feels right and push back on what doesn’t.
  2. ~1 week to let the vault be rendered and published, probably pseudonymously at first or under just “Hiro” with no last name.
  3. ~1 week to sharpen the flagship essay from existing notes.
  4. ~3 weeks to build the starter kit, which is exactly the kind of design-engineer project he already enjoys.

That’s roughly 6 weeks of actual work, spread across however many months he wants. No urgency. No outside money committed. If any of it starts feeling off, stop.

Zak’s role here

To set expectations up front: Zak wrote this folder as a thoughtful read, not a partnership offer. He’s heads-down on his own project (Forgeloop) and isn’t available to co-build, run GTM, or otherwise carry any of this forward operationally. The _os/ folder is an honest strategic read you can use as input — zero obligation in either direction. See from-zak for the full framing. If a smaller ask comes up later (a read on a draft, a second pair of eyes), the door stays cracked open for that.
